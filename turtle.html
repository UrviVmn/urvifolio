Research Ideation Solution

Turtle voice assistant

A calm, ambient voice companion that unifies hotel-room controls and services into one natural interaction layer. With voice as the fixed medium, the challenge was to find a context where it could create meaningful human value.

Designing within a technological constraint

How we chose: modality compatibility. Interactions are more efficient when input and response share the same sensory modality. Where we chose: the hotel room, where verbal communication is already familiar.

~250 crore domestic tourist visits in India in 2023. Every stay begins in an unfamiliar room, yet nearly half of hotels leave guests to figure out in-room technology on their own. 70% of hotels say guests prefer a human, yet what guests struggle with most is basic: lighting, Wi-Fi, TV and AC.

Why is this needed?

A guest’s request shouldn’t have to travel through layers to be heard. A well-informed VUI connects request to resolution: not replacing hospitality with automation, but making hospitality work better.

Design brief

A Voice User Interface can function as a unified, ambient interaction layer within the hotel room, allowing guests to express intent naturally while remaining flexible, context-aware and unobtrusive. The interaction should adapt to the user, not the other way around.

The enchanted way of doing

Without the intervention, a guest may have to search, guess, call, wait, or give up on a service. In the desired reality, a guest feels oriented, supported and in control. Standing in the way: unclear room logic, no guided onboarding, fragmented controls, telephone friction, speech hesitation, no instant answers, fear of misuse and delayed service loops.

Why the room, not reception?

Placing the VUI in the room brings assistance closer to the moment of need, helping guests navigate an unfamiliar environment with greater confidence and less effort.

Personas

Yash, 64, senior adult guest: “Guide me slowly and reassure me that I’m doing it right.” Arjun, 24, the self-serve explorer: “It would be nice if the room just understood what I need.”

Meet the turtle

A quiet companion in your hotel room, always available, never intrusive. It informs, requests and escalates, expresses through light and touch, controls the room by voice, and anticipates guest preferences.

How do we make assistance feel welcome, not watched?

An unobtrusive faceless form, intentional interaction, and familiarity before entry through a turtle at the door. System persona: polite, calm, efficient, empathetic, multilingual, invisible when not needed. The turtle’s protective shell symbolises a sense of home that travels with it.

Feedback systems

Auditory, visual, haptic, environmental, listening state, error feedback and adaptive feedback that changes with night, day and shared rooms.

Intent tree

The voice flow starts by understanding what the guest is trying to do, not the exact words, mapping each request to the closest intent: welcome and orientation, room usage and controls, services, complaints, reception and human help, safety and emergency, hotel and local information, and fallback handling.

Ideation and form development

A turtle feels approachable and non-intimidating. Its compact, speaker-like shape says it is something you can talk to, while the hexagonal shell becomes the light-based display.

Light animations

Resting state, starting, listening, task being done, error and reading information, plus touch feedback.

Special cases: VUI behaviour

Using failures as information, from guests speaking at neighbouring doors to network loss and misheard commands.

Chat-bot prototype

Built in Voiceflow, with welcome and orientation, intent blocks and guard rails.
